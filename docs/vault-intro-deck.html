1 / 7 · the problem

Your AI forgets everything.

Every new chat starts from zero. You re-explain the project, the decisions, the gotchas — again. Whatever it figured out yesterday? Gone.

We gave ours a memory. Here's how.

2 / 7 · the vault

A folder of notes the AI keeps.

Plain-text notes that live right in the project: what we decided and why, what's been done, how things fit together. The AI reads them before it works — and updates them after.

vault/
├── decisions/  why we chose things
├── sessions/   what's been done, day by day
└── features/   how each part works

You never maintain these notes — the AI does. And they're in the repo, so the whole team's AI shares one memory.

3 / 7 · see it once

Ask, and it already knows.

> /v-ask why do we soft-delete orders?
Decided in March: legal needs 90 days of order history.
Full reasoning in decision note #12.

One command. It answers from the team's notes — and changes nothing.

4 / 7 · the commands

Four commands. Two questions.

Just asking?

/v-ask

Answers from the notes. Only looks — never changes anything.

Making a change?

small/v-do

No fuss, straight to it. Doesn't write notes unless you ask.

normal/v-work

Asks you before doing anything; writes down what it learned after. Not sure it's small? Pick this — it spots small jobs and skips the ceremony by itself.

big / risky/v-team

A panel of AI critics argues about the plan first — and drafts the test plan. About twice the cost. For decisions that are expensive to undo.

5 / 7 · the point

Tuesday decides. Thursday remembers.

Tuesday

You run /v-work on exports; along the way the team settles on CSV over Excel. The why gets written down — automatically.

Thursday · new chat

A colleague asks /v-ask why CSV? Instant answer. No re-explaining. Their session builds on yours.

That's the whole trick: what one session learns, every later session knows.

6 / 7 · why bother

What you get back.

Less re-explaining.

Finding a past decision in the notes takes a fraction of the reading that digging through the code does — measured ~96% less on this repo.

Decisions survive.

The "why" behind choices stops living in one person's head.

New teammates start smart.

Their AI already knows the project on day one.
